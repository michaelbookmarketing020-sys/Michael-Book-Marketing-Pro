Frequently Asked Questions

Honest Answers to Common Questions From Authors.

A straightforward guide to how I work, what professional book marketing can and cannot do, and how to get started on a project together.

A professional book marketer helps authors give their books the best possible chance of being discovered, read, and recommended. The work includes planning a marketing strategy, improving how a book is presented, running advertising campaigns, reaching out to readers and reviewers, optimizing book listings on retail platforms, and measuring what is working so campaigns can be refined over time.

The role combines creative judgement, platform knowledge, data analysis, and communication. It is less about any single tactic and more about bringing every element together in a way that reflects the book and the audience it is written for.

Yes. A meaningful portion of my work is with first-time authors who are preparing to launch a debut book or are navigating the publishing world for the first time. I will walk through the fundamentals with you, explain the decisions that matter, and build a marketing plan that fits your budget and goals.

Starting with a clear foundation often saves significant time, money, and frustration later in your author career.

Yes. Self-published and independently published authors make up a significant part of the authors I support. The services I offer are well suited to the needs of independent authors who are responsible for their own marketing and want a professional partner for part or all of that work.

In many cases, a thoughtful backlist campaign produces better long-term results than a rushed launch campaign ever could.

Audience identification is a mix of structured research and informed judgement. I look at comparable titles, reader reviews, popular keywords and categories, bestseller lists in the relevant niches, and the conversations happening in reader communities. From that, I build a picture of who the book is for and where those readers tend to spend their time.

That picture then informs every other decision, from advertising targeting and newsletter outreach to the language used in book descriptions and promotional graphics.

No. Anyone who guarantees a specific number of book sales is not being honest with you. Book sales depend on many factors, including the book itself, the market conditions at the time, the genre, the price, the cover, reader reception, and the quality and consistency of marketing activity.

What I can guarantee is careful planning, professional execution, honest reporting, and ongoing refinement of campaigns based on real performance data. Those are the ingredients that give a book its best chance of selling well over time.

No. Bestseller rankings are influenced by many factors that no marketer can fully control, including the activity of competing titles at any given moment. I have been fortunate to support twenty authors who have achieved bestseller status in their categories, but those results came from a combination of strong books, considered strategy, and favourable timing.

If someone promises you a bestseller ranking in advance, treat that promise with caution.
